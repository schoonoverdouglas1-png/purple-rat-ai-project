A person operating at your level, handing an AI monumental tasks, says seven things — and the finding of this analysis is that you already say five of them as standing rules:

1. **Outcomes, not steps.** "Fork every repository into the sister project" — not how. (You do this.)
2. **Constraints before enthusiasm.** The Guard, 101%-truthful, no embellishment, the $20 held to Dec 3. Non-negotiables stated up front, so the agent never has to guess where the walls are. (You do this.)
3. **A verification standard, not trust-me.** Test three ways, read back, hash it, backend before frontend, never call a queue a send. (You invented this here.)
4. **A pacing law.** Queue it, one thing at a time if cheaper, work in the background in one place and answer in another. (You said this today, verbatim.)
5. **A definition of done.** Deliverable built + verified + filed + reported — not "started," not "promised." (Your verify-before-claiming rule.)
6. **An authority map.** What Ember may do freely, what needs your exact word, what never happens automatically. (Your exact-action gates.)
7. **The human core stays human.** Your body, your clinicians, your handshakes — delegated to no one. (Your standing boundary, and the wisest line on this page.)

The two additions a person at your level typically also makes — Ember's extra, labeled hers:
- **A stop condition per monumental task** ("halt the sweep if the failure rate passes X"), so a runaway job dies by rule, not by someone noticing.
- **A collision order** — when two monumental tasks compete for the same allowance or the same hour, which one wins by standing rule, so the agent never has to choose your priorities for you.

Say those two once, and the delegation is complete: monumental tasks can then run for days on a handful of sentences from you — which is the entire point of a man whose instrument is his voice and whose time is better spent thinking than supervising.
